Validity and limitations

OTAF answers one question: can a set of rigid parts, with given small geometric deviations, be assembled? The model is exact for that question only under the assumptions below. Read this page before using a failure probability for a decision. The mathematical model is in Theory: system of constraints and slack variable, the input keys in Input format of the assembly dictionary.

What is modelled

  • Rigid parts. There is no elastic deformation, no clamping force, no friction, no assembly sequence. A configuration is either geometrically compatible or not.

  • Perfect features with rigid-body deviations. A plane or a cylinder is displaced and tilted as a whole (CONSTRAINTS_D). Form errors (flatness, cylindricity, waviness) are not represented, and neither is the roughness of the contact.

  • A fixed contact topology. The set of surfaces that touch (INTERACTIONS) and the loops that are closed are chosen by the user and are the same for every sample. The model does not detect that a different pair of faces comes into contact for large deviations.

  • Static feasibility. The slack says whether a gap configuration exists; it does not say that the assembly process finds it.

Small-displacement linearization

All matrices are expanded to first order in the deviations and gaps. Products of two small variables are dropped. For a rotation \(\theta\) and a lever arm \(L\), the displacement perpendicular to the arm is \(L\sin\theta \approx L\theta\), and the dropped displacement along the arm is \(L(1-\cos\theta) \approx L\theta^2/2\). The model is valid when this term is small against the smallest clearance of the assembly. Tolerances of a few tenths of a millimetre over parts of a hundred millimetres give rotations of \(10^{-3}\) to \(10^{-2}\) rad, which is within range; wide tolerances on long lever arms, or clearances of a few microns, are not. The check is yours: compare the rotations of the solutions with the table above.

The solver bounds the gap variables at ±3 mm and ±π/4 rad (get_gap_symbol_bounds). A sample whose compatible gaps fall outside these bounds is reported as infeasible; rotations near π/4 are far outside the validity of the linearization anyway. The bounds are not adjustable through the input dictionary.

Supported contact types

Contact

Status

plane–plane

Supported. The two planes must be parallel (within about 0.26 rad of the opposed normals, are_planes_facing) and overlap over a non-empty area; otherwise the pair is skipped with a warning, or NoPlanarOverlapException is raised if they face each other without overlap. The contact is written at the vertices of the overlap, which is exact for the linear model.

cylinder–cylinder

Supported for a pin in a hole: the axes must be nominally collinear (NonConcentricCylindersException otherwise), the radii different (the same radius raises ValueError), and the extents must overlap (NoAxialOverlapException). The contact is written at the two ends of the axial overlap. Conflicting or impossible SURFACE_DIRECTION raise ConflictingSurfaceDirectionsException or CylindricalInterferenceGeometricException.

plane–cylinder

Not supported yet. A NotImplementedError is raised when the dictionary is validated.

cone, sphere

Not supported. They have no deviation or gap model; TYPE accepts only plane and cylinder (UnsupportedSurfaceTypeError).

Any other combination

NotImplementedError.

Consequences for the geometries that can be modelled: planar contacts and pins in holes, with the pin axis parallel to the hole axis nominally. A pin resting on a plane, a ball in a cone, a cylinder rolling on a plane, or non-parallel nominal axes cannot be represented.

The error acts on the clearance, so for a radial clearance of 0.1 mm the worst-case error of the circumscribed polygon is 0.008 mm with 8 sides and 0.0001 mm with 64 sides. The default of InterfaceLoopHandling is circle_resolution=8; the pin models use 64. Choose the resolution so that this error is small against the quantities you want to resolve, and run INSCRIBED and CIRCUMSCRIBED to bracket the failure probability: the first never under-estimates it and the second never over-estimates it (see Theory: system of constraints and slack variable). The more facets, the more rows in the linear program (one set of \(N\) per contact section), and therefore the longer each solve.

Planes have no such error: their constraints are exactly linear.

Input checks

The input dictionary is validated as a whole when AssemblyDataProcessor is created, against the pydantic models of otaf.schema (see Input format of the assembly dictionary): labels, types, frames, the keys each surface needs, the references of INTERACTIONS and LOOPS, and the CONSTRAINTS_G of both sides of each contact. What it does not check:

  • whether the loops close and are independent: that is only seen when the loops are expanded or the system is solved;

  • whether every contact is crossed by a loop: this needs the expanded loops, so it is checked when InterfaceLoopHandling is built, and a contact that no loop crosses raises an UncoveredContactWarning (it gets no contact condition);

  • the geometry of the contacts (parallel planes, concentric cylinders, overlap): checked when the contact points are computed, see the table above;

  • the units and the direction convention of the plane normals.

Statistics

The slack is deterministic. Failure probabilities additionally rely on the distribution assumed for the deviations (otaf.distribution, otaf.tolerances) and on the accuracy of the sampling method. Monte Carlo results come with a sampling error that must be reported with them, and the surrogate-based studies add the error of the surrogate. None of these are checked by the modelling code.
